## 1. Executive assessment

- **New since Sunday’s cutoff:** Meta launched equity offerings and outlined a larger future financing program. The financial question is when capital becomes available and what shareholders surrender through dilution, rather than the headline size alone.
- **A contrasting cash-flow signal:** Verizon reported stronger profitability and raised guidance, even as revenue disappointed expectations. Its AWS agreement provides evidence of AI spending reaching connectivity suppliers beyond accelerators and cloud operators.
- **Strongest supported opportunity:** Infrastructure providers that turn demand into cash can benefit without having to establish a new consumer AI business. Contract economics and delivery costs still matter; announced value alone cannot establish investment returns.
- **Principal risk:** Financing capacity and strategic investor interest can sustain construction before end-user returns are demonstrated. That creates a gap between funded capacity and profitable utilization. This is analyst judgment, not a forecast of a funding shortfall.

The window runs from October 25 at 09:04:36 EDT through October 26 at 09:15:11 EDT. Sunday’s earnings-preview themes are background; today’s financing launch and earnings release are new events.

## 3. Material news

**Meta’s financing launch.** The [issuer announcement](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Announces-Launch-of-Offering-of-Depositary-Shares-Representing-Series-A-Mandatory-Convertible-Preferred-Stock-and-Offering-of-Class-A-Common-Stock-in-Connection-with-Forward-Sale-Agreements/default.aspx) describes approximately $35 billion of concurrent offerings: $22.5 billion of common stock through forward sale agreements and $12.5 billion of mandatory convertible preferred depositary shares. A separate, approximately $60 billion Class C at-the-market program is contemplated for the first quarter of 2027; no agreement has been entered into and establishment is uncertain. The common forward sales provide no initial proceeds to Meta; settlement is contemplated by November 19, 2027. These are plans and a launch, not $95 billion of cash already received.

[CNBC’s full article](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/26/meta-stock-offering-ai.html), published this morning, reports that Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom expressed interest in purchasing up to $5 billion of shares. The issuer explicitly calls the indications nonbinding. Strategic interest can improve financing confidence, but it should not be treated as a completed purchase, a chip order or independently proven demand for AI services.

**Verizon and AWS.** Verizon announced an agreement valued above $2 billion. [Reuters, syndicated by AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/verizon-lifts-annual-forecasts-signs-110318000.html), says Verizon will provide high-capacity fiber connectivity for AWS data centers. The reviewed disclosures do not establish a revenue-recognition schedule or contract margin. Consequently, the agreement cannot be added to quarterly revenue or translated into an AWS earnings estimate.

| EPS / adjusted EPS | $1.25 / $1.31 | +6.8% / +8.3% YoY |

| Operating cash flow / free cash flow | $11.5bn / $7.8bn | +2.2% / +12.1% YoY |

First-nine-month operating cash flow of $29.9 billion less capital expenditure of $11.9 billion equals reported free cash flow of $18.0 billion. Capital returned was $12.8 billion, including $8.8 billion of dividends and $4.0 billion of repurchases. Net unsecured debt was $123.7 billion; reported net unsecured debt/adjusted EBITDA was 2.3 times. These definitions do not represent an all-in GAAP debt ratio. Full-year adjusted EPS guidance is now $5.04–$5.06.

Reuters attributes revenue expectations of $34.74 billion to LSEG and postpaid phone additions of 209,439 to Visible Alpha. Actual additions were 406,000. Calculated differences are a **1.27% revenue miss** and a **93.85% subscriber beat**. These are externally reported estimates, not directly retrieved consensus series. Strong subscriber acquisition alongside weaker equipment sales makes revenue mix and customer profitability more informative than a single beat/miss label.

Meta’s financing release reiterates management’s $135–$145 billion 2026 capital expenditure expectation, $97 billion of operating cash flow in the first nine months and approximately $82 billion of quarter-end cash, equivalents and marketable securities. Full-year capex and nine-month cash flow cover different periods; subtracting them would not produce valid annual free cash flow.

## 6. Industry fundamentals and competition

The two announcements suggest different routes to capturing AI economics: owning large compute facilities and selling essential connectivity to facility operators. Neither route eliminates execution risk. The useful comparison is incremental cash earned after construction, operation and financing costs, rather than nominal project scale.

Reuters reports lower Verizon device upgrades as customers retain phones longer after subsidy reductions. That supports a narrow observation about this carrier’s sales mix. It does not establish an industry-wide collapse in device demand. Likewise, chipmakers’ proposed equity participation supplies no verified market-share change or evidence that enterprise software budgets are expanding. Customer concentration and exposure to a small group of large infrastructure buyers remain analytical questions to test against subsequent disclosures.
